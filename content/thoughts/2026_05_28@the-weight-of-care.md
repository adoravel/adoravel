the worst days are the ones where my own head gets too loud to ignore. when you're this
aware of where you are, where you came from, and the systems you built to survive it,
reflection stops helping. it just paces the cell.

lately i've been trying to parse a specific kind of friction. psychology and philosophy
used to be a hobby, and the reading gave me a vocabulary for my own thoughts, an exactness
i didn't have before. it also made me a hyper-critical observer of my own behaviour. i can
tell you exactly how irrational a tendency is. that changes nothing. knowing the mechanics
of a spiral doesn't keep you out of it; it means you get to watch yourself fall in 4k.

i value autonomy above almost everything. possessive, controlling behaviour disgusts me,
so my brain punishes me for wanting even transient, exclusive attention. divided attention
feels unsafe. attention i had to ask for feels unearned. if it feels demanded, it violates
the thing i care about most, the freedom of the people i love. and if i don't get it, the
silence detaches me from everything.

so it turns into guilt. i think the discomfort stems from an internalised judgment that my
wanting to be chosen, to be held in focus, is intrusive. stifling. remorse follows every
time. when you carry trust issues and a history of neglect, your default assumption is
that your needs are a burden, that having feelings at all is a failure of discipline, that
it's on you for being like this.

but behaviour isn't immutable. when someone shows you, again and again, that your presence
isn't toxic to them, that wanting to share their time is just what care looks like and not
obssession, the guilt starts to crack. i've seen otherwise now. i'm cautiously confident
the anxiety will shrink, and that it won't drag the same remorse behind it. i'm still
figuring myself out.

this is the exhausting part of changing. survival keeps most people too busy to look
inward at all, which is the same paradox i wrote about before: survival versus
self-assessment. there's rarely energy for both. either you spend it on the repair and
risk disconnecting from everyone around you, or you let life run and become a half-assed
productivity machine, blissfully unaware of what's underneath.

growing doesn't have to be lonely or punishing. thank you for getting me through this. i
love you so so so so much. thank you. like, i can't even put into words how thankful i am
